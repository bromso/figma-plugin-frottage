A Slow Week on the Coast

By Maren Holt · 14 minute read

Chasing the morning light

The first thing visitors notice is the light. It arrives low and sideways, turning wet stone the colour of honey, and for twenty minutes every window along the quay burns like a lantern.

Local cooks will tell you the morning is the only honest time to shop. By ten the best produce is gone, the tourists arrive in loud, cheerful clusters, and prices drift upward with the heat.

None of this is a secret, exactly. It is simply unadvertised, passed from neighbour to neighbour in the way that useful things often are, and that is part of what makes it worth the early alarm.

Walk inland and the road begins to climb. Within an hour the sea becomes a bright strip behind you, and the terraces take over, stacked like the pages of an enormous, patient book.

Farmers here have worked the same slopes for centuries. Their walls are dry stone, their tools are modest, and their patience with questions from strangers is, on the whole, remarkable.

“You do not find the place by arriving. You find it by staying long enough to be bored, and then to be surprised.”

What to pack

  • Sturdy walking shoes with real grip
  • A light wool layer for the evenings
  • A refillable bottle and a paper map
  • Cash, for the market and the ferry
  • A book you are willing to finish

Go in the shoulder season, book nothing you cannot cancel, and leave one day entirely empty. That empty day is usually the one you remember.
